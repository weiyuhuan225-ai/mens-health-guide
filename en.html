How to Masturbate Well

Focus less on numbers, more on your body and daily life.
A clear guide to hygiene, friction, frequency, and emotional concerns. Whether to masturbate, do it less often, or not at all is your choice.[1]

01 / FREQUENCY Frequency

How often?
There is more to consider.

Some people masturbate frequently, some occasionally, and some not at all. Consider these four areas when deciding whether to make changes.[1][2]

Open “What you can do” on each card for practical suggestions.
01

Any physical discomfort?

Pain, broken skin, numbness, and persistent swelling all deserve attention.

What you can do

Stop stimulation and allow the area to recover. Seek medical care if symptoms persist, worsen, or keep returning.[19]

02

Can you stop when needed?

Can you postpone or stop when you need to? Have you repeatedly tried to cut back but been unable to?

What you can do

Notice triggers such as boredom, anxiety, or the content you browse. Seek a professional assessment if a continuing loss of control is disrupting your life.[2]

03

Is it crowding out daily life?

Look at the time it actually takes, especially time needed for sleep, study, and work.

What you can do

If it regularly keeps you up late, set your bedtime first. Briefly tracking the time spent and the circumstances may help you identify what to change.

04

What exactly is troubling you?

Is there physical discomfort, or is the main worry “Have I damaged myself?”

What you can do

Separate actual consequences from judgments about yourself. Guilt and a real loss of control can coexist; both deserve thoughtful attention.[27]

The goal of making changes: restore comfort, personal control, and everyday functioning. This guide does not calculate an “ideal frequency” or an “addiction score.” Tracking circumstances and planning your time are everyday management suggestions, not a validated treatment program.
02 / PRACTICE Safer habits

Be gentle.
It matters more than force or duration.

Reliable research does not establish an ideal pressure, speed, or number of minutes for everyone. Practical steps focus on reducing irritation and injury, and listening to your body's signals.

Clean gently.
No repeated disinfecting needed.

Maintain hygiene without making your skin more sensitive.

Wash your hands and clean gently

Use warm water to clean the external genitals. Avoid repeated scrubbing, alcohol, disinfectants, and fragranced products that may irritate the skin.

Only move the foreskin as far as is comfortable

If the foreskin retracts comfortably, gently clean underneath, then return it to its usual position. Do not force it if it is tight or painful.

Let existing discomfort settle first

If the skin is already damaged or painful, do not keep stimulating it to check whether it has healed.

Remember: the aim is clean, comfortable skin—not making the skin “sterile.”[17][18][19]

Let comfort guide you.
Reduce friction and pressure.

Pain is not something to push through.

Be gentle and avoid excessive force

Avoid sustained heavy pressure, twisting, or forceful bending. Stop if you notice pain, broken skin, significant numbness, or swelling.

Reduce dry friction when needed

Choose a lubricant suitable for the body area and compatible with any products you use. Stop using it if it causes irritation, such as burning or itching.

Check compatibility with latex

Avoid oil-based lubricants with latex condoms because they can weaken latex.

Remember: you do not need to ignore discomfort just to last longer or reach ejaculation.[17][19][20][21]

Clean up gently.
Let your body recover naturally.

You do not need to try again immediately to prove that everything is normal.

Gently remove residue and pat dry

If you have a foreskin, make sure it is back in its usual position. Masturbation does not require special disinfection afterward.

Pause if friction has caused discomfort

Allow the area to recover before considering more stimulation. Get assessed if discomfort persists, worsens, or keeps returning.

Allow for the refractory period

Not wanting to continue or being unable to get another erection immediately after ejaculation can be part of normal recovery. This varies widely between individuals.

Remember: a brief feeling of relaxation or sleepiness does not mean your body has been “depleted.”[13][17][18]

Toys are optional.
If you use one, follow its care instructions.

Different materials need different cleaning methods.

Check for damage first

Look for surface cracks, rough edges, or other damage. Avoid items that could scrape or injure the skin.

Follow the cleaning, drying, and storage instructions

Do not assume every powered toy can be submerged in water, or that the same heat treatment or disinfectant is safe for every material.

Check lubricant compatibility

Follow the manufacturer's guidance for the toy's materials. Stop using it if you feel discomfort.

Remember: check cleaning requirements and material compatibility before focusing on price or extra features.[22]

03 / EVIDENCE Evidence

Do not fear being “drained,”
or treat masturbation as a health requirement.

Open each card to see what the research examined and what it could not establish. These figures come from studies; they are not a way to calculate your personal risk or set a recommended frequency.

Short-term changes ≠ lasting harm
Association ≠ causation
Ejaculation frequency ≠ masturbation frequency
Common question · Does it drain testosterone and energy?

Thinking of ejaculation as ‘using up testosterone’ is misleading.

Current research does not justify a universal abstinence schedule.

Small crossover experimentsCannot establish long-term effects

In a 2021 pilot trial, 8 men completed the study. It measured short-term hormone changes and did not show that masturbation causes a sustained fall in testosterone. Another study of 10 men found higher testosterone after three weeks of abstinence, but this does not show that longer abstinence makes you healthier. [5][6]

The English-language paper often cited for a ‘day-seven rise’ was retracted in 2021 for duplicate publication. This does not mean its data were proved fraudulent. The small sample also limits what it can establish: it does not justify a ‘once every seven days’ rule. [7]

Common question · Does less semen mean reduced fertility?

Semen volume is not a direct measure of fertility.

Time between ejaculations affects several measures. Volume alone cannot tell the whole story.

Systematic reviews / Meta-analysesPopulations and measures differ

Two reviews published in 2024 included 85 studies and 13 studies with 2,315 participants, respectively. Longer intervals often increase semen volume and sperm concentration; in some analyses, shorter intervals are associated with better sperm movement or less sperm DNA fragmentation. The reviews may include overlapping studies, so their samples cannot simply be added together. [8][9]

The usual pre-test requirement of 2–7 days without ejaculation standardizes semen sampling; it is not a recommended schedule for everyday masturbation. Follow the specific advice you receive when trying to conceive or providing a sample. [10][11]

Common question · Does masturbation always cause erection problems?

Sexual function depends on several factors.

Your past masturbation frequency alone cannot establish permanent damage.

Observational research / Clinical guidelinesNot an individual diagnosis

A study involving 3,586 men found a weak and inconsistent relationship between masturbation frequency and erectile function. Age, anxiety or depression, and chronic illness also matter. [23]

European Association of Urology (EAU) guidelines note that some atypical masturbation patterns may be associated with delayed ejaculation. Assessment should also consider medication, psychological and relationship factors, and physical causes. Evidence for some behavioral treatments remains limited. [3][4]

Ongoing difficulties deserve assessment, including those that occur only during sex with a partner. Do not stop prescribed medication or take hormones without medical advice.

Common question · Does it always help sleep—or always reduce fitness?

Your experience matters. The evidence is still limited.

Look at your sleep routine first. Masturbation is not an insomnia treatment.

Diary research / Small trialsFindings are mixed

A 2023 diary study analyzed 2,076 entries from 159 people and found no significant association between masturbation and better sleep. A 2025 pilot study with just 14 participants (7 men) found improvements in some objective measures, but no significant differences in time to fall asleep, total sleep duration, or subjective ratings. [15][16]

A meta-analysis of physical performance also did not support the claim that sexual activity generally reduces physical fitness. However, it included only 133 participants, almost all men; its findings cannot be treated as direct evidence about studying or exam performance. [14]

Putting a frequently misread number back in context

‘21 times a month prevents cancer’
goes beyond what the study can tell us.

A cohort study of 31,925 men found that more frequent ejaculation was associated with a lower risk of a prostate cancer diagnosis. The association mainly concerned low-risk disease.

The study did not establish separate effects of masturbation and sex with a partner, and other differences between participants could still explain the association. 21 per month marks a study category, not a health target. [12]

Study hazard ratio (HR): 0.78

Recalled ejaculation frequency at ages 40–49: ≥21 times a month
compared with 4–7 times a month; 95% confidence interval: 0.69–0.89.

0.78 is not your probability of developing cancer.
Association does not establish causation, and it does not create a frequency you need to meet.

Short-term changes ≠ long-term outcomes

A single hormone change cannot establish a lasting change in physical health.

Occurring together ≠ causing each other

An association between a behavior and a symptom does not show that the behavior caused the symptom.

One measure ≠ your overall health

Semen volume, frequency, and individual test results cannot replace a full assessment.

04 / MIND & HABITS Habits and emotions

Frequency, guilt, and loss of control
need separate attention.

Start with the specific problem you want to change. You do not need to label yourself ‘damaged’ or ‘addicted’ first.

01 · Where your time goes

Masturbation and endless scrolling are different activities.

If the problem is endless searching and scrolling, reducing easy access to that content and protecting bedtime may address it more directly. This is everyday habit management, not a treatment plan.

Frequent pornography use alone does not establish a disorder. What matters more is repeatedly being unable to stop, having it crowd out daily life, or continuing when you do not want to. [26]

Does pornography necessarily affect sexual function?

A study using both cross-sectional and longitudinal analyses did not find that pornography use consistently predicted changes in erectile function over time. The evidence does not support an inevitable cause-and-effect relationship, nor does it rule out difficulties in individual cases. [24]

02 · What you tell yourself

Separate what happened from how you judge yourself.

Some distress comes from a conflict between behavior and personal beliefs. You can keep your values and seek support for anxiety or guilt. Guilt and an actual loss of control can also coexist. [2][27]

A worst-case interpretation ‘Doing it once means I've ruined myself.’
A more useful question ‘What is actually uncomfortable? What needs adjusting?’

Research on conflicts with personal values mainly concerns pornography use. Its findings cannot automatically be applied to every concern about masturbation.

03 · What is happening in your relationship

Masturbation and dissatisfaction may be intertwined.

A systematic review found that, in many male samples, solitary masturbation was associated with lower sexual satisfaction. But people who were already dissatisfied may also have masturbated more to meet their needs. Association alone cannot tell us which way the relationship runs. [25]

If you have a partner, talk about each other's needs, boundaries, and specific concerns. Frequency alone cannot tell you how healthy the relationship is.

04 · Ongoing difficulty with control

You can seek help without diagnosing yourself.

ICD-11 focuses on persistent loss of control that causes significant distress or impairment in daily life. A high sex drive, frequent activity, or distress arising entirely from moral self-judgment is not enough on its own for a diagnosis. Compulsive sexual behavior disorder is classified as an impulse control disorder.[2]

Clinicians look for a pattern lasting several months, but you can seek help as soon as it causes significant distress. You do not need to wait six months. Professional support aims to help you regain control over your behavior.

If repeated attempts to cut back have not worked, or sleep, study, or work is clearly affected: consider speaking with a mental health professional or a sexual medicine clinic for an assessment of physical health, emotions, medication, and behavior.

A review of 24 studies, including 4 randomized controlled trials, found preliminary support for interventions such as cognitive behavioral therapy. The evidence remains limited; there is no ‘reset program’ that works for everyone. [28]

05 / WHEN TO GET HELP When to seek medical care

If something feels wrong,
know where to turn next.

Use this section to decide when to seek help, not to diagnose yourself. These conditions are not necessarily caused by masturbation. Do not dismiss an emergency as a normal aftereffect.

Stop stimulation first

Mild and improving

Mild irritation from friction that eases after you stop.

Give the area time to recover. Get assessed if symptoms persist, worsen, or keep returning. [17][19]

Book an appointment

Persistent or recurring

Recurring pain, unusual discharge, painful urination or ejaculation, or blood in semen.

Book an appointment with a primary care clinician or urologist. Seek prompt care if symptoms worsen significantly or you feel generally unwell. Blood in semen usually does not mean a serious condition, but its cause should still be checked. [18][29]

Get emergency care now

Do not wait with these warning signs

  • After an injury: sudden severe pain, rapid swelling or bruising, or an abnormal change in shape.[20][21]
  • The foreskin gets stuck after being pulled back and cannot be returned to its usual position. [18]
  • An unusual erection lasting around 3–4 hours, whether painful or not. [30]
  • Sudden, severe testicular pain; testicular pain with nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain; or testicular pain that lasts more than 1 hour or continues while you are resting.[31]
Seek emergency care sooner for severe pain or other serious symptoms. Do not wait 4 hours. If you have sickle cell disease, a painful erection lasting more than 1 hour needs emergency care. Do not try to end an abnormally prolonged erection by continuing to masturbate.

If you are unsure how serious your symptoms are, or they are getting worse quickly, contact local medical services promptly. These time thresholds are for urgent assessment; they do not mean it is safe to continue stimulation for hours.

What if I feel unwell for days after every ejaculation?

If you repeatedly feel unwell for several days and it affects daily life, tell a clinician about the pattern. Postorgasmic illness syndrome (POIS) has been described in the medical literature, but research is still based mainly on case reports and small studies, with limited evidence on causes and treatment. Brief, ordinary drowsiness is not enough for this diagnosis. Do not self-medicate based on case reports you find online.[32]

Make your own choices.
Listen to your body.

The goal is to understand your behavior, make your own choices, and adjust when needed. If discomfort or loss of control persists, seek appropriate help.
