# How to Masturbate Well

English | [中文](README.md)

**Read online: [English edition](https://weiyuhuan225-ai.github.io/mens-health-guide/en.html) · [中文版](https://weiyuhuan225-ai.github.io/mens-health-guide/)**

A plain-language, visual sexual health guide for adult men, adapted from a narrative literature review with 32 references.

## What the guide covers

- Four areas to consider: physical comfort, personal control, daily functioning, and distress.
- Care before, during, and after masturbation, plus guidance for using toys.
- Findings and limitations of research on testosterone, fertility, erectile function, sleep, and prostate cancer.
- The distinctions between pornography use, guilt, relationship concerns, and persistent loss of control.
- Clearly visible guidance on when to seek medical care, with 32 traceable references.

## Read or run locally

Open `en.html` for English or `index.html` for Chinese. Each page includes its own HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and bibliography. No installation is needed. Both pages have a language switch at the top.

The site uses no third-party fonts, scripts, or analytics. External links are provided for reading source material and visiting the repository. Interactive content does not score, diagnose, collect, or upload personal health information.

Layouts adapt to phones and desktop screens. The care tabs support keyboard navigation, and the page respects reduced-motion preferences and supports browser printing. Selecting an in-text citation reveals and focuses its bibliography entry. All four care stages and the full bibliography remain readable when JavaScript is disabled.

GitHub Pages serves the site from the root of the `main` branch. The `.nojekyll` file enables direct static publishing.

## Scope and evidence limitations

This is general health education for adults, not an individual diagnosis. It does not prescribe a universal masturbation frequency, duration, or abstinence schedule, and does not calculate addiction or injury scores.

Sources were checked on October 9, 2026. The underlying review is narrative: no systematic review protocol was preregistered, and no exhaustive screening or formal evidence grading was performed. Short-term physiological changes do not establish long-term health outcomes. Observational associations do not establish causation.

One cited item is a retraction notice, included to explain the status of evidence behind a widely repeated claim. The retracted paper is not used as a basis for a health recommendation. Original publications remain the property of their respective copyright holders; this project provides citations and source links.
